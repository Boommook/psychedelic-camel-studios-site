## Game Concept Development

Over the past week, we met as a team multiple times to determine the concept for our game. At first, we compiled a document to brainstorm ideas, such as plots, mechanics, and effects. Then, we wrote our Treatment Document by filtering through our brainstormed ideas and molding them into a realistic concept for the game. The document features an overview of the gameplay, the narrative of the game, planned mechanics and effects, necessary assets, concept art, and references. This document serves as an initial outline of our game and our plan of implementation.

Additionally, Trevor created an asset list document that names the assets we plan to implement and details about each one, depending on the type of asset. For example, a character asset needs geometry, textures, and rigging, so the details are: high-poly, retopo’d mesh, unwrapped mesh, texture set, and rigged and finalized FBX. We will keep the list updated throughout the term, documenting the dates that work started and ended for each asset, and the status of completion and implementation within the engine.

After our class on Monday, the team thought about the critiques that were provided in class, and how we can embrace them to bring success to our game.

## Technical Work

The first step on the technical side was determining the version of Unreal Engine that we plan to use. We decided to use Unreal Engine 5.8.4 and set up a Git repository for source control as we develop the project. The version number was decided upon after investigating the UE5 version on the school machines, as some of our team members will be developing on those. Cole created the Git repo, which was configured with Git LFS, as advised by our professors, and features a README that briefly explains the game concept, configuring the repo, and our planned workflow.

> https://github.com/Boommook/paranoia

Additionally, Cole developed the paRAnoia website, which serves as a hub for our game and its details. The site was developed using Next.js, TypeScript, and TailwindCSS, and provides details about our game’s concept, our team, and our progress throughout the term through weekly blog posts. The website includes a homepage introducing our game, an About page explaining the narrative, gameplay, and development team, a page for our development blogs (where you are reading this right now :) ), and a resources page which will host builds, footage, concept art, and other project-related assets.

> https://psychedelic-camel-studios-site.vercel.app/

Multiple members of the team also began exploring Unreal Engine. Chloe spent time familiarizing herself with Unreal Engine and its workflow, while Domenic investigated the Blueprint system. Meanwhile, Trevor experimented with Unreal Engine and 3ds Max. He played around in Unreal Engine and 3ds Max (even though he and Morgan will be using Maya) to familiarize myself with the tools. Within the tools, he tested out grey boxing, the built in 3rd person player, importing/exporting models, and lighting.

## Artistic Work

This week, the art team began developing concept art for our game’s characters, props, and environments. Morgan sketched our main character, Twizz, experimenting with various art styles, both in and out of her comfort zone, to find one that best suits our vision for the game. The initial design had a more cartoonish appearance before transitioning towards a semi-realistic style, using character models from Phasmaphobia as reference. Morgan also created concept art for several props. The props she drew are a little simple, outside of that psychedelic flower, but she drew them with fun ideas in mind. For example, during the high, the clock could spin, or the elevator door could open to a claustrophobic space that only shrinks the longer you’re in there. The flower was drawn with the dreamcore/weirdcore aesthetic in mind. A lot of dreamcore imagery contains placing eyes on things like mushrooms, TVs, the sky, etc and dreamcore is often accompanied by backgrounds that fit that liminal space vibe. Our team wants the rooms the main character walks through to be inspired by liminal space settings that you’d find in media like the Backrooms, and so she kept that in mind when creating this weird little flower guy.

## Puzzle Work

The puzzle/experience team discussed some inspirations and what sort of an experience they want to offer to the players, and wrote it down. Most of us found inspiration from our own experiences, liminal spaces/experiences we have had, media that we’ve consumed etc. and tried to draft out how the experiences would be.

## Inspirations

### Berenis

I found great inspiration from the movie Paprika (2006) directed by Satoshi Kon: While it
doesn’t include psychedelics, the dream machine, which is under research, starts blurring reality and dreams when it gets intercepted by a party with negative intentions. It is up to the main characters to get to the root of all chaos while trying to decipher what is real and what is dream-state. The first scene we are introduced to what is going on blends in the transition extremely well as the head of that research group slowly starts to use silly, or unrelated words to only then expose that his reality and world state are merging at an increasing velocity. So many scenes afterwards where the sane characters also start descending into being suspicious about everything they interact with. One scene that stuck with me was when the main character Atsuko tries to follow a dream-world avatar to what seems to be a theme park, only to almost fall off the tall apartment where the suspected research lab fellow, as she tries to jump what she sees
to be a fence in the theme park.

### Brandon

As we have begun the process of making our game, finding good examples of inspiration from other media has been one of the biggest things to help us start developing what we want the game to feel like. A few of the ideas brought up in class have really stuck with me, specifically the Psycho Mantis boss fight in MGS and PT. These ideas stuck with me because a system that could read your system for info to give the player a shocked and uneasy feeling. I also liked the example of PT as it is a demo that takes place in one hallway the entire time, where you repeatedly do the same thing with the environment getting increasingly horrifying. Both of these examples show ideas I’d like to incorporate in some capacity, as we already have plans to have a part of the dorm that will be “infinitely” repeating, and I want to try and lean into the liminal and creepy aspect of that.

### Domenic

Some of the inspirations of weird and interesting things that came to mind were Transcendental Cha Cha Cha by Tom Cardy and the Moss by Cosmo Shelldrake.

### Chloe

As we were developing our ideas, I watched a portion of the LSD Dream Simulator walkthrough which gave me a lot of ideas of how the trip can be expanded in terms of hallucinations and possible cutscenes in a simple gameplay setting. While we were brainstorming designs and puzzle mechanics, I was also inspired by the movie Before I Wake, a movie about a child whose dreams and nightmares become part of his reality. It’s like a fantastical movie until it turns into a horror film, but I think there’s a lot of correlation between that storyline and how this trip goes badly. I think having fantastical elements that eventually turn to be a little disturbing or scary would help enhance the overall experience. I also started looking into Unreal engine and how to work with it since this will be my first project working on it. Another gameplay experience that we can draw from perspective-wise, is Phasmophobia. The way that events and hauntings will begin with or without prompting is a good mechanic to integrate into our game, as we don't want the player to be able to nor to try to avoid the effects of the goop. Having the trip hallucinations occur both with and without prompting will allow the player to enjoy the experience more rather than play to avoid these events.
