## Technical Work

After Alpha, we recognized that our game was not playable and in a solid state. We immediately created a spreadsheet of features to be added and tasks to be done, the category, assigned team member, priority level, and status. This helped us stay on track for the week.

### Cole

Over the past week, I have largely taken on constructing our environment. To begin, I made a prototyped infinite hallway, which works by combining a few different techniques. First, the player must enter an activation trigger at the start of the hallway, which then enables a moving wall. As the player walks down the hallway, the wall moves at the same rate, and eventually the player enters the loop trigger, and is teleported back to near the activation trigger. Multiple extra rooms are also spawned at the end of the hallway, past the loop trigger, to make it seem as though the rooms go on forever. Initially, this prototype just used the greybox walls, floors, and furniture, but after completing the other environment decorating, I updated the infinite hallway to use the same models as the rest of the dormitory.

My major work this week consisted of sourcing free 3D assets and adding them to our environment, to lighten Trevor’s load. I found a few kits, including a horror house and office. Additionally, I searched for some individual assets that I could not find in any kits, such as beds and a closet. I then implemented these new assets in our first level, starting with the bathrooms and common room. I then moved on to designing the rooms. To ensure that we have variability in our game, I created a BP_Base_Room with three messiness levels: clean, normal, and messy. For each model that I added to the room, I assigned it to a messiness level that controls its visibility. Next, I made five child rooms based around themes: plants, studious, gamer, stoner, and normal. I used assets from the kits and other individual assets I found to decorate these rooms. The normal room just extends the base room. Finally, I added the rooms to the level, randomizing both the theme and messiness of each one.

### Brandon

My tasks this past week have been designing and writing up the moment-to-moment gameplay as well as designing some of the puzzles for the player to experience through each level. In order to give the player an objective that makes sense for an RA, we decided to make a basic few tasks that need to be done for each floor, such as close all dorm doors, check bathrooms, and turn off any appliances in the common room. These basic tasks are used to drive the player to go to specific places in the level, with the puzzles and experiences happening due to the player having to check those places. This coming week, my main goal will be to implement the puzzles into the level with all of the events properly working and triggering when they are supposed to.

## Artistic Work

### Trevor

In the past 2 weeks, I finished modeling Twizz in ZBrush as well as giving him placeholder textures and UV unwrapping him. The UV unwrapping on the face wasn’t optimal, so I polished the UVs in 3ds Max. With the unwrapped model complete, I added Twizz into 3ds Max and scaled him correctly to around 180 cm. I then created 18 unique morph targets, modeling them in ZBrush and implementing them into 3ds Max. I then finally rigged Twizz using CAT. I gave him weight paints and exported him into Unreal Engine. Using a 3rd person player, I retargeted Twizz to the Unreal mannequin which allows the player to control him. Unfortunately, I realized I forgot to mirror the weight paints, so I went back into 3ds Max and fixed them up for the most part. I also tested moving his rig in Unreal but it is breaking at the moment.
